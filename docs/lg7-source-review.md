## Consolidation and source use

At the user's request, the original 14 issues are consolidated into 10. The
four merged pairs are summons/service, initial/consent drafting exercise,
security gateways/residence, and security discretion/timing and stifling.
All substantive topics remain, with one duplicated concluding step combined.
Surviving issue IDs remain stable; aliases resolve the four retired issue URLs
to their combined pages. Display codes change with the shorter issue list.

The exam-facing text now states the rules and reasoning directly, with legal
citations rather than references to annexes, slides or page numbers. This audit
retains source locations and version conflicts separately. The recheck expands
the evidence sequence, solicitor audience and District Court adaptation,
PD16.1 hearing-bundle index requirements, contractual severance and alternative
periods, precise customer/employee classes, confidentiality categories and
unlimited-duration confidentiality pleading, and the factual reasoning in
Carmon, Vianini, Dongguan Harris and Waddington. Case facts are illustrations,
not fixed thresholds or substitutes for applying the legal test.

## Decisions affecting exam answers

- O.32 r.3 concerns **service**, despite “filed” in Annex (1) para.7.
- O.42 r.3 distinguishes the date an order takes effect from subsequent
  drawing up and sealing. Annex (1) para.23 should not override the reproduced
  rule or the slides on this point.
- The outline's exercise dates take priority over the older A2 slides.
  Its weekday/date labels conflict, so the notes do not calculate deadlines
  from those weekday labels.
- The defendant's proposed concessions are not an established agreement by
  the plaintiff or an admission of past misuse. Injunctive relief requires
  the appropriate consent-summons route, not assumed paper entry under O.42 r.5A.
- Delivery-up scope, recipient and the start of the 21-day period are not fully
  supplied. Drafting prompts retain explicit blanks. No completed model answer
  or particular costs disposition is invented.
- Companies Ordinance **s905** is used; B2's s907 headings and the predecessor
  s357 references are distinguished. The 2025 foreign-company definition is
  included without treating re-domiciliation as immunity under every limb.
- Case-specific discounts, payment periods and concessions are examples,
  not automatic rules. Submissions in Carmon and Dongguan Harris are not
  promoted into holdings. Counterclaim/common-issue costs need separate analysis.
- Default on security does not automatically produce judgment on the merits.
- The referenced negligence casefiles (Batch B pp.110–114, 127–128 and Batch C
  pp.41–65) were not supplied. Their exercises are not reconstructed.
- Detailed ex parte injunction merits are outside this addition. O.29 r.1 is
  supplied only as procedural context for the uploaded drafting exercise.
