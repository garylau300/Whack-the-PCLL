Problem A: extra units, rooftop room and a late architect’s letter

Facts: P buys Flat C. OP shows seven units; assignment plan shows nine. The building has a rooftop room. V replies two days before completion that the flat is non-structural and there has never been enforcement. P considers terminating immediately.

Structured answer:

  1. Separate showing and giving. The OP discrepancy reasonably requires inquiry. A late generic opinion may fail Kok / Active Keen even if a substantive defence later emerges. State the contractual/implied timing and what the letter actually explains.
  2. Locate the extra units and rooftop works. If the unit combination is confined to other flats, Active Keen / Hong Kong Central Hospital may support the subject title. Roof/common-structure works may create collective exposure; do not apply that protection without tracing location and liability.
  3. For the rooftop room, Mariner means “not in the building” can defeat s.41(3) exemption independently of structural character. Heavy concrete raises the MTR point too. Inspect approval/minor-works evidence.
  4. Assess Spark Rich safe-disregard risk against concrete enforcement history; decades of inaction and an “unlikely” letter are not automatically enough. Check lease linkage (Giant River) and DMC restrictions independently.
  5. Request OP/approved plans, work chronology, BA correspondence, structural/drainage evidence, lease/DMC and actual cure plan. If V proposes demolition, ask whether approvals and completion timing are realistic and whether P loses significant area/value/function under Cashew / Max Smart.
  6. Decide timing. Curable defect alone normally leaves V until completion. A serious matured showing-title breach is separate; an irremovable fundamental defect may permit earlier termination. Identify the basis rather than saying P may rescind simply because UBW exists.

Likely conditional conclusion: V’s response is inadequate if it does not address the additional units, roof liability and all relevant facts. P’s entitlement to terminate depends on whether the disclosure failure has matured into repudiation and/or whether lawful substantial cure before completion is impossible.

Problem B: family estate, undervalue gift and occupier

Facts: A died with a tenancy-in-common share. The administrator transferred it cheaply to his daughter. A’s widow lives there and says she funded the deposit. The daughter sells, producing only probate papers and the earlier assignment.

Structured answer:

  1. No survivorship for the deceased’s tenancy-in-common share. Require death/grant, will or intestacy basis, transfer form and actual beneficiary entitlement (Sun Sek Haw / So Mariko).
  2. Grant of administration is authority to administer, not proof that the daughter alone owned the asset. Consider s.55 / Feerni, improper estate purpose and serious undervalue (Lam Sik Shi); the ordinary administration-sale presumption may be displaced.
  3. Investigate the widow’s contributions and intention; analyse RT/CICT and any relevant advancement presumption. Do not assume that she inherits the entire share or that her occupancy alone establishes a trust.
  4. Occupation and her express claim put P on notice. P cannot plausibly claim equity’s darling while ignoring it (Wong Chim Ying; Flying Mortgage). Assess an acquisition mortgage’s priority separately if relevant (Cann).
  5. Examine gift/undervalue/fraud routes, with the applicable bankruptcy dates and creditor facts. Low consideration is evidence, not automatic proof of every statutory ground.
  6. Demand valid distribution/authority evidence, beneficiary approvals or court authorisation as appropriate, and a genuine release/confirmor conveyance of any widow’s interest. Probate papers do not release her independent equitable ownership.

Conclusion: the daughter has not shown an unencumbered title merely by proving registration. Multiple possible claimants need distinct, substantive answers; an indemnity cannot by itself transfer any missing beneficial estate.

Problem C: 2015 company release by a foreign-bank attorney

Facts: a legal mortgage was “released” in 2015. A company with three directors signed by one director. It purported to act for a foreign bank under an unsealed POA. The register shows the mortgage entry vacated. P contracts in 2026.

Structured answer:

  1. Registry vacation does not establish release (Siu Wing Yee). Identify whether the document is a s.56 receipt or a deed; the applicable formalities differ.
  2. A 2015 document is less than fifteen years old for a 2026 S&P, so no corporate s.23A(2) age protection. Post-2003 means no s.23A(1) historical-authorisation route either.
  3. Under current CO, one director of a three-director company does not satisfy the statutory signature route merely by signing. Check whether another lawful execution/authority route or s.23 appearance is actually available; the company has not met the sole-director shortcut.
  4. Independently inspect the foreign bank’s POA execution and scope. s.26(1) LARCO may accommodate a foreign unsealed corporate power if proved valid under incorporation law; unsupported assertion is insufficient (Li Yuen Ling / Wang Zhidun).
  5. Check the POA’s power to release the particular security and non-revocation/protection. A perfect foreign-law opinion about the power would not alone cure a badly executed release by its donee.
  6. Obtain a fresh properly executed release or other effective cure. Verify the exact mortgage/property/shares and every executing capacity (Au Wai Ming).

Conclusion: a vacated entry and repayment evidence do not resolve two independent execution defects. Show an actual effective discharge and its supporting authority, rather than only no complaint since 2015.

Problem D: expired charging order, hanging S&P and IO injury claim

Facts: a 2019 charging order was not renewed. A 2020 registered S&P has no assignment. An IO faces a personal-injury claim. V offers $100,000 retention and says everything is “old”.

Structured answer:

  1. CO: establish expiry and the precise dates of P’s registration and any renewal. An intervening qualifying purchaser may gain s.3(2) LRO protection (Wong Chung Keung); expiry alone does not extinguish debt/order (Si Tou Choi Kam).
  2. Hanging S&P: obtain termination, settlement, damages and lien evidence. Hansun / Guang Xin may remove the prior buyer’s purchase interest, but a continuing genuine lien needs separate treatment. A stale register entry is not proof of abandonment.
  3. IO litigation: apply s.17 BMO, assess damages/costs, resources/insurance and enforcement exposure. A personal claim against IO can burden P unlike an ordinary personal debt claim against V (Lucky Health).
  4. V must provide particulars and a reliable risk estimate in time (Kan Yui Man; Lo Yee Man; Gigabillion). “Only a claim” or “old matter” does not answer the statutory liability route.
  5. Evaluate the retention against each burden separately. It may be irrelevant to a priority-protected CO, insufficient for an unextinguished prior proprietary interest, and arbitrary against an unknown major IO claim. Apply Page 12’s coverage/enforceability/resale checklist.

Conclusion: V may have a defensible answer to the expired CO, but that does not answer the prior purchaser’s rights or exceptional collective litigation. Success on one requisition does not prove the entire title.

Problem E: survivor sells a flat and separate parking space

Facts: H and W acquired as joint tenants. H mortgaged his interest secretly, then died. W sells the flat and parking space to separate buyers. The DMC prohibits separation; the parking space is in a different location from the plan.

Structured answer: investigate whether the mortgage severed the joint tenancy (Mak Pui Ki) and thus whether W actually acquired all the estate by survivorship. Do not apply the charging-order comparison indiscriminately. Follow any deceased share through estate administration. Next analyse the DMC restriction (Lo Chi Wai; Dragon Top) independently of W’s ownership. Finally compare the actual parking location with the conveyed shares/exclusive-use plan (Fan Tony) and obtain reinstatement or a valid corrective instrument. A death certificate alone, physical use of the relocated bay and willingness to indemnify do not answer all three issues.

First learn Page 01 and the question sequence. Then study beneficial ownership (Page 05), UBW/collective liability (Pages 06–07), and execution/POAs (Pages 09–10) as connected clusters. Use the remaining pages for chain-specific problems and cures. Attempt the five hypotheticals without the structured answers; afterwards check whether each conclusion addresses evidence, substantive title, cure and timing. Practise applying dates rather than memorising a fixed 2011 threshold.

For a timed answer, spend space on disputed mechanisms and material facts. Do not recite every case in the pack; select one leading rule and the most relevant comparison for each issue. End each issue with the evidence/cure required and a conditional or definite conclusion supported by the given facts.
