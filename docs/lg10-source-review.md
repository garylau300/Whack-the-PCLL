## Accuracy decisions

- **Combined document:** O.15 r.2(1) requires adding the defendant's
  Counterclaim to the Defence. O.18 r.3(3), cited loosely in the lecture,
  actually governs the plaintiff's combined Reply and Defence to Counterclaim.
  The notes distinguish both requirements.
- **Specific traverse:** a bare general denial is not a substitute for
  essential traverses. The r.13(6) saving rule requires the nature of the case
  on the relevant issue, not merely the existence of a Defence. Chapple's
  particulars qualification does not remove the substantive response duty.
- **Office-entry exercise:** instructions use 2024 records; slides 48–49 use
  2023. The adapted example follows the instructions. Consent to entry was not
  converted into consent to removing records, and no general legal right of
  self-help was invented.
- **Diamond exercise:** the claim, instructions and PDF consistently use
  February 2025, while some slides and SmartArt use 2024. The notes use 2025,
  distinguish the buyer's $168,000 allegation from the actual $100,000 deposit,
  and preserve the difference between a refused refund offer and repayment.
  The sample's repeated paragraph number is identified rather than copied.
  Pronouns do not supply an assumption about the principal's gender.
- **Set-off:** O.18 r.17's reference to unascertained money does not dispense
  with substantive legal or equitable eligibility. A Counterclaim is not
  automatically a set-off; the slides' mutuality, liquidation, connection and
  contractual-exclusion qualifications are retained.
- **Additional Counterclaim party:** O.15 r.3(1) still starts with a defendant
  who Counterclaims against P. The connected-relief limb was not converted into
  an unrestricted freestanding Counterclaim against a co-defendant. O.16
  r.8(2) is assessed before selecting a notice.
- **Packaging:** the contract price is $52,500 plus $2,000 delivery, totalling
  $54,500. The sample's erroneous delivery “to the Defendant” is corrected to
  delivery to the buyer. Its reversed mitigation sentence is explained using
  the instructions: the objection is the Italian order rather than available
  local replacements. No conclusion about every box was inferred from the
  partially inspected and signed delivery evidence.
- **Contractual protections:** clause 5 says a defective-goods claim must be
  brought within 14 days, clause 6 concerns the specified excluded loss, and
  clause 7 uses total price **paid**, not payable. The notes preserve the wording
  and distinguish pleading reliance from establishing enforceability. No
  statutory-validity rule absent from the materials was supplied as an answer.
- **Interest:** the sample's $54,500 price, 15% contractual rate and stated
  29 December 2024 start are identified. The due date and late-payment trigger
  are distinguished for a fresh draft; no accrued-interest total without an
  end date was invented.
- **Sample captions and verification:** the source contains inconsistent
  District Court action numbers and an incorrectly attributed corporate
  signatory in the claim. The notes require checking actual process and the
  correct corporate capacity rather than guessing a final caption.
- **Leave:** the original defendant's r.1(2) exception concerns writ and issue
  before Defence service. Rule 2(1) permits ex parte application with the Court
  able to direct a summons; the outline's abbreviated summons description was
  not treated as the full mandatory procedure.
- **Directions:** D's mandatory r.4(1) application is distinct from TP's
  fallback. Rule 4(2)'s 7 days runs from intention to defend; rule 8(4) substitutes
  14 days from service of the existing-party notice. These are fallback
  thresholds, not invented fixed deadlines for D's application.
- **Default:** rule 5(1)(b) requires a main default judgment against D and
  distinguishes contribution from other relief with leave. The outline's
  “only contribution” shorthand was read against the full supplied rule.
  Neither an automatic indemnity nor automatic execution follows merely from
  TP's silence. Rule 5(2) can apply to D's default too.
- **Judgment and execution:** rules 4(3)(a), 5 and 7 provide different judgment
  mechanisms. Rule 7(2) restricts execution for the specified contribution or
  indemnity before discharge, with a leave exception; it does not prevent entry
  of judgment or cover every independent damages award automatically.
- **Survival and direct relief:** Stott's procedural independence is paired
  with the outline's substantive qualification where there is no liability left
  to pass on. P's direct judgment requires defendant joinder and its own claim;
  disclosure or party status as TP does not supply that claim. Joinder after
  limitation expiry is separately qualified under O.15 r.6(5)–(6).
- **Further parties:** r.9(3) substitutes writ and 14 days after the time limited
  for acknowledging the notice against TP. The original D's pre-Defence-service
  rule and an actual early acknowledgement were not substituted for that clock.
- **Contribution offers:** r.10 defers disclosure until liability and amount
  are determined. The split-trial exception permits only fact and date at the
  specified liability-costs stage, not amount. No automatic costs penalty was
  invented.
- **Unseen case-file bundles:** the outline references Contract B19–B64 and
  Negligence B75–B76, but those bundles were not uploaded. The notes use the
  supplied B28 extract and examples without inventing unseen clauses, accident
  details or a contractual indemnity. Personal addresses and incidental contact
  details in the exercises were excluded.
